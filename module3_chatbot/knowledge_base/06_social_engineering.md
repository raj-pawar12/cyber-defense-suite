# Social engineering

## Idea
Social engineering attacks people instead of computers. Attackers exploit trust, fear, urgency, curiosity and respect for authority.

## Common techniques
Pretexting invents a believable story, such as a caller pretending to be from IT support. Baiting leaves infected USB drives or offers free downloads. Tailgating follows an employee through a secure door. Quid pro quo offers help or a prize in exchange for information. Impersonation pretends to be a boss, bank or government officer, as in business email compromise.

## Defences
Slow down when a message creates urgency. Verify requests through a second channel using a number you already trust. Never share OTPs or passwords, even with someone claiming to be staff. Follow company procedures for access and payments. Report suspicious contacts so others are warned.
